# C03 source decision — historical shorthand

Decision date: 2026-08-30. First-run decision:
`EXCLUDED_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`; `C03_PRODUCTION_READY=false`.

## Scientific role

C03 must be a scholarly, machine-readable diplomatic transcription of
historical shorthand in running text. A sign dictionary, isolated examples,
or ordinary Latin abbreviation corpus does not satisfy this role.

## Sources considered

| Source | Access and format | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| British Library Add MS 21164, *Lexicon Tironiarum*, 10th c. | Stable BL catalogue record; manuscript lexicon, not an acquired machine-readable running-text transcription | Reject: wrong text genre and no suitable transcription snapshot. |
| Martin Hellmann, *Supertextus Notarum Tironianarum* | Online sign dictionary based on Schmitz (1893) | Reject: lexical inventory, not shorthand running text. |
| BDD diplomatic abbreviation representation | Local TEI, CC BY 4.0, reproducible | Retain as C02 only: scribal abbreviation is not shorthand in general. |

No candidate found in the audit simultaneously meets genre, transcription,
versioning, provenance, and licensing requirements. Replacing C03 with BDD or
a Tironian dictionary would narrow the candidate definition after the study
design and is prohibited.

## Derived transcription feasibility

A manual transcription is not admitted for this freeze. No bounded running-text
source with a preregistered sign inventory, page/line sampling frame, and a
second independent transcriber was available. Creating those inputs now would
require a separate paleographic project and could not satisfy independent
validation or an evidence-based minimum of 5,000 USC tokens. This is a source
availability exclusion for the first run, not evidence from comparative
metrics and not a permanent rejection of C03.

Sources checked: BL catalogue `https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002091538`;
`http://www.martinellus.de/snt2/n/incipit.htm` (accessed 2026-08-30).
