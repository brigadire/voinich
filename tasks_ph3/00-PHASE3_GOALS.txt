PHASE3_GOALS
Voynich Generative Structure, Information Residual,
and Mechanism Identifiability


======================================================================
1. MOTIVATION
======================================================================

Phase I established that the Voynich Manuscript (VM) exhibits
strong constrained formation and non-trivial statistical structure,
while tested simple transformation mechanisms were insufficient
to identify its origin.

Phase II tested whether this structure could be explained by
external-memory mechanisms motivated by Giovanni Fontana,
historical shorthand/abbreviation, selective extraction,
natural-text controls, or simple null mechanisms.

The authoritative Phase II result is:

    BEST_SUPPORTED_CLASS = INCONCLUSIVE

    MECHANISM_IDENTIFICATION_FROM_F2 =
        NOT_IDENTIFIABLE

The principal methodological limitation was:

    direct model/target coverage = 3 / 13 CORE metrics,

all belonging to one edit-related family.

At the same time, questions inherited from Phase I remain unresolved:

    Can Voynichese be described by a compact formal grammar?

    How much of the corpus is predictable from such a grammar?

    What structured information remains after grammar-conditioned
    regularities are removed?

    Can constrained meaningful and constrained message-free
    generation be distinguished?

Phase III addresses these two limitations jointly.


======================================================================
2. CENTRAL OBJECTIVE
======================================================================

The objective of Phase III is to determine:

    whether the observed structure of Voynichese can be explained
    by a compact generative system,

and:

    whether competing generating mechanisms become identifiable
    when they are required to reproduce manuscript-scale structure
    rather than token-stream statistics alone.

Phase III therefore studies:

    STRUCTURE
        +
    INFORMATION
        +
    GENERATION
        +
    IDENTIFIABILITY.


======================================================================
3. TWO RESEARCH LINES
======================================================================

Phase III consists of two initially independent research lines.


LINE A:

    VOYNICH GENERATIVE GRAMMAR
    AND INFORMATION RESIDUAL


LINE B:

    SYNTHETIC MANUSCRIPT GENERATION
    AND MULTI-FAMILY FINGERPRINT COVERAGE


The lines must initially remain independent.

They may be combined only after each line has produced
a frozen and independently validated result.


======================================================================
4. LINE A — CENTRAL QUESTION
======================================================================

Line A asks:

    How small a formal generative system is sufficient
    to reproduce the measurable local structure of Voynichese?

This is NOT initially a linguistic grammar claim.

"Grammar" means:

    a formally specified probabilistic or deterministic
    generative system over observable transcription units.

The grammar must not assume that:

    glyph = letter;
    token = word;
    line = sentence;
    token component = morpheme;
    generated sequence = natural language.


======================================================================
5. GRAMMAR LEVELS
======================================================================

Investigate at least three potentially distinct levels:

G1 — TOKEN FORMATION

    Which combinations of glyphs/components form valid
    or probable tokens?

G2 — TOKEN SEQUENCE

    How does surrounding token context constrain
    token choice and token form?

G3 — STRUCTURAL CONDITIONING

    How do line position, boundaries, locus,
    local regime and higher structural state
    change those distributions?

Do not assume beforehand that these require
one unified grammar.


======================================================================
6. GRAMMAR DISCOVERY
======================================================================

Candidate grammars must be inferred from training data.

Evaluation must use held-out VM data:

    V_train → G

followed by:

    G → prediction(V_test)

and/or:

    G → synthetic corpus.

No grammar may be selected solely because it reproduces
statistics measured on the same data used to construct it.


======================================================================
7. GRAMMAR MODEL CLASSES
======================================================================

Phase III should compare multiple formal model classes
rather than selecting one representation in advance.

Candidate classes may include, subject to preregistration:

    finite-state models;
    probabilistic finite-state models;
    variable-order Markov models;
    probabilistic grammars;
    rule-based token formation models;
    hierarchical/state-conditioned models;
    other compact generative descriptions.

Model complexity must be explicitly measured.

No neural language model should serve as the primary
scientific grammar unless an interpretable complexity
and comparison contract is defined beforehand.


======================================================================
8. MINIMALITY
======================================================================

The objective is not maximum fit.

The scientific quantity of interest is the trade-off:

    explanatory power
        vs
    model complexity.

Define:

    G_min

as the smallest validated model that reproduces
a preregistered set of structural properties
within declared tolerances.

Possible complexity measures may include:

    number of states;
    number of rules;
    number of free parameters;
    description length;
    grammar entropy.

The metric must be frozen before final comparison.


======================================================================
9. HELD-OUT PREDICTION
======================================================================

Grammar quality must not be evaluated only
by token likelihood/perplexity.

Evaluation should include:

    unseen-token handling;
    token-form distribution;
    edit-family structure;
    lexical/paradigm structure;
    local transitions;
    repetition;
    positional distributions;
    boundary behavior;

and other directly applicable frozen
Fingerprint V2 properties.

The grammar must reproduce joint structure,
not merely token frequencies.


======================================================================
10. GENERATIVE VALIDATION
======================================================================

After grammar freeze, generate synthetic corpora:

    G → V_synthetic.

Then compute applicable Fingerprint V2 metrics:

    F2(V_synthetic)

and compare against held-out VM:

    F2(V_synthetic)
        vs
    F2(V_test).

A grammar that predicts likelihood well
but fails the structural fingerprint
must not be considered sufficient.


======================================================================
11. INFORMATION RESIDUAL
======================================================================

After obtaining a validated grammar G,
define the information unexplained by G.

Conceptually:

    V = G + R

where:

    G = predictable generative structure;
    R = residual information.

The objective is to characterize R,
not to assume that it represents plaintext.


======================================================================
12. RESIDUAL ENTROPY
======================================================================

Estimate quantities such as:

    H(V)

    H(V | G)

and the reduction:

    ΔH_G = H(V) - H(V | G).

Where appropriate, use predictive
or description-length equivalents
rather than naïve entropy estimators.

The estimator and nulls must be frozen
before scientific evaluation.


======================================================================
13. RESIDUAL STRUCTURE
======================================================================

Test whether residual R retains reproducible dependence at:

    token scale;
    neighboring-token scale;
    line scale;
    page scale;
    folio scale;
    section scale;
    positional scale;
    local-regime scale.

The critical distinction is:

    low residual entropy

versus:

    structured residual information.


======================================================================
14. INFORMATION CHANNEL QUESTION
======================================================================

Ask:

    After accounting for the generative grammar,
    does the remaining sequence contain statistically
    structured degrees of freedom?

Possible outcomes include:

A.

    Grammar explains most measurable structure
    and residual resembles matched nulls.

B.

    Grammar explains token formation,
    but substantial structured residual remains.

C.

    Grammar itself cannot compactly explain
    the observed structure.

These outcomes have different implications
for the competing hypotheses.


======================================================================
15. MEANINGFUL VS MESSAGE-FREE GENERATION
======================================================================

Phase III must attempt to operationalize the distinction between:

    constrained information-bearing generation

and:

    constrained message-free generation.

This must NOT depend on knowing a plaintext.

Possible discriminators must be formulated
as statistical hypotheses and validated
against controls before application to VM.


======================================================================
16. MESSAGE-FREE GENERATORS
======================================================================

Construct preregistered message-free generators
capable of reproducing the frozen grammar.

They may use:

    stochastic rule choice;
    constrained token formation;
    local state;
    positional state;
    hierarchical state;

but must contain no hidden plaintext/message.

Generate multiple independent corpora:

    M_1, M_2, ... M_n

from independent seeds.


======================================================================
17. FINGERPRINT-PRESERVING NULL GENERATION
======================================================================

A central Phase III experiment should ask:

    Can independently generated meaningless corpora
    repeatedly reproduce the VM fingerprint?

Formally:

    M_i ~ G_null

and test whether:

    F(M_i) ≈ F(V)

for many independent i.

This directly addresses the unresolved
Phase I question of meaningless generation.


======================================================================
18. REPEATABILITY CRITERION
======================================================================

A message-free explanation becomes substantially stronger if:

    many independently generated documents

reproduce:

    multiple independent fingerprint families

without:

    target-specific tuning;
    seed selection;
    post-hoc parameter adjustment.

One successful synthetic document
is insufficient evidence.


======================================================================
19. LINE B — CENTRAL QUESTION
======================================================================

Line B asks:

    Can competing mechanism classes generate
    manuscript-scale structure sufficient for
    genuine multi-family comparison with VM?

The goal is to remove the primary
Phase II bottleneck:

    3 / 13 CORE direct coverage.


======================================================================
20. SYNTHETIC MANUSCRIPT OBJECT
======================================================================

Define a generic manuscript representation
that can contain at minimum:

    glyphs;
    tokens;
    physical lines;
    line boundaries;
    pages;
    folios;
    recto/verso relation;
    loci;
    sections/regimes;
    2D positional coordinates where justified.

Every structural element must have provenance:

    GENERATED_BY_MECHANISM

or:

    INTRODUCED_BY_ASSEMBLER.


======================================================================
21. NO SYNTHETIC SEMANTICS
======================================================================

The assembler must not silently invent
scientifically meaningful manuscript structure.

For example:

    splitting every N tokens into lines

does not automatically create evidence
about physical-line structure.

Metrics dependent on assembler-created
structure must remain classified separately
unless the mechanism itself generates that structure.


======================================================================
22. GENERATIVE HIERARCHY
======================================================================

Where possible, mechanisms should generate structure through:

    mechanism → token
    mechanism → line
    mechanism → page
    mechanism → folio
    mechanism → manuscript

rather than:

    token stream → arbitrary formatting.

The scientific question is whether manuscript organization
can emerge from or be required by the generating mechanism.


======================================================================
23. STRUCTURAL SCALE
======================================================================

Candidate mechanisms should be evaluated separately at:

    token;
    local sequence;
    line;
    page;
    folio;
    manuscript

scales.

A mechanism may succeed at one scale
and fail at another.

This distinction must remain visible.


======================================================================
24. TARGET COVERAGE
======================================================================

The principal quantitative goal of Line B is to increase:

    DIRECT CORE COVERAGE.

Phase III should aim for:

    multiple independent F2 families

rather than merely a larger number
of correlated metrics.

The desired progression is:

    3/13 one-family
        →
    multi-family direct coverage.


======================================================================
25. COVERAGE IS NOT FIT
======================================================================

Increasing coverage is a methodological success,
not evidence for a hypothesis.

A mechanism that becomes directly measurable
on 10/13 CORE metrics but strongly disagrees
with the VM is scientifically more informative
than one matching 3/13.


======================================================================
26. COMPETING GENERATING CLASSES
======================================================================

Line B should retain at least the major classes
already frozen by Phase II:

    NATURAL_TEXT
    SIMPLE_NULL
    FONTANA_EXTERNAL_MEMORY
    HISTORICAL_SHORTHAND
    SELECTIVE_EXTRACTION.

Phase III may additionally admit:

    FORMAL_GRAMMAR
    MESSAGE_FREE_GENERATOR

after Line A freezes them.

New mechanism classes must be preregistered
before target comparison.


======================================================================
27. NATURAL-TEXT MANUSCRIPT CONTROL
======================================================================

Natural text must be promoted from a token-stream
control to an appropriately structured document control.

The purpose is to determine which F2 properties arise from:

    language;

versus:

    ordinary document organization;

versus:

    VM-specific structure.

Formatting must be historically or procedurally justified,
not tuned to the VM.


======================================================================
28. SHORTHAND EXTENSION
======================================================================

Phase II disfavored one BDD abbreviation tradition.

Phase III must not reinterpret this as:

    shorthand globally rejected.

If shorthand is revisited, use independent
historical traditions with paired or otherwise
recoverable expansion evidence.

This should test:

    cross-tradition stability

which Phase II could not establish.


======================================================================
29. EXTERNAL-MEMORY EXTENSION
======================================================================

Fontana mechanisms may be extended to manuscript scale
only when the extension is explicitly classified as:

    SOURCE_ATTESTED

    SOURCE_CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION

or:

    GENERALIZED_EXTERNAL_MEMORY.

Do not attribute modern hierarchical constructions
to Fontana without historical evidence.


======================================================================
30. TRAJECTORY PROBLEM
======================================================================

Phase II lacked a valid frozen Fontana
before→after F2 trajectory.

Phase III should attempt to construct
properly preregistered transformation trajectories
for mechanism classes where the concept is meaningful:

    input
        →
    mechanism
        →
    manuscript-scale output.

Endpoint similarity alone remains insufficient.


======================================================================
31. NULLS
======================================================================

Every mechanism family requires
mechanism-appropriate matched nulls.

Nulls should preserve nuisance properties such as:

    length;
    token frequency;
    line length;
    page size;
    positional distribution;
    hierarchy size;

as appropriate.

A mechanism is not supported merely because
it is closer to VM than another complex mechanism.


======================================================================
32. LINE A / LINE B FIREWALL
======================================================================

Before Line A freeze:

    Line B must not tune manuscript mechanisms
    using grammar results.

Before Line B model freeze:

    Line A must not choose grammar according
    to which mechanism class it favors.

The two lines should converge only after
their primary models are frozen.


======================================================================
33. CONVERGENCE
======================================================================

After both lines are independently frozen,
construct a common hypothesis space:

    H_N:
        natural-language document

    H_F:
        Fontana/external-memory mechanism

    H_S:
        historical shorthand

    H_A:
        selective extraction

    H_G:
        formal generative grammar

    H_M:
        constrained message-free generation

plus declared nulls.


======================================================================
34. MULTI-FAMILY COMPARISON
======================================================================

For each frozen class compute the largest
honest direct F2 intersection.

Compare by:

    metric;
    family;
    structural scale;
    transcription;
    corpus;
    seed.

Do not collapse all evidence immediately
into one scalar distance.


======================================================================
35. IDENTIFIABILITY — SECOND TEST
======================================================================

Repeat the Phase II identifiability question
only after expanded coverage:

    MECHANISM_IDENTIFICATION_FROM_F2_V3.

Possible outcomes:

    CLASS_IDENTIFIABLE
    FAMILY_IDENTIFIABLE
    MULTIPLE_CLASSES_COMPATIBLE
    NOT_IDENTIFIABLE.

A repeated NOT_IDENTIFIABLE under substantially
expanded multi-family coverage would itself be
a stronger scientific result than Phase II's
coverage-limited non-identifiability.


======================================================================
36. GRAMMAR IDENTIFIABILITY
======================================================================

Separately determine whether the grammar itself
is identifiable.

If several structurally different grammars
produce indistinguishable held-out predictions
and fingerprints:

    GRAMMAR_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE.

Do not select one merely because it is
more intuitively linguistic.


======================================================================
37. COMPRESSION / DESCRIPTION LENGTH
======================================================================

Where statistically appropriate, compare:

    raw description length of VM

against:

    grammar + residual

and:

    mechanism + state + residual.

Conceptually:

    L(V)

versus:

    L(G) + L(V | G).

This provides a principled measure
of how much structure the model actually explains.

The coding scheme must be frozen
before final target evaluation.


======================================================================
38. INFORMATION ACCOUNTING
======================================================================

For each mechanism/model explicitly account for:

    information in observable symbols;
    information in model parameters;
    information in external state;
    information in convention/key;
    information in residual;
    information lost by transformation.

This extends the recovery accounting
developed in Phases I–II.


======================================================================
39. TRANSCRIPTION ROBUSTNESS
======================================================================

All applicable primary results must be checked
against both authoritative VM transcriptions.

A Phase III conclusion should distinguish:

    TRANSCRIPTION_STABLE
    DIRECTION_STABLE
    TRANSCRIPTION_SENSITIVE.


======================================================================
40. HIERARCHICAL HELD-OUT TESTS
======================================================================

Random token splits are insufficient.

Where possible, use progressively stronger held-out units:

    token;
    line;
    page;
    folio;
    section.

This tests whether a grammar or mechanism
generalizes across manuscript structure
rather than memorizing local distributions.


======================================================================
41. TARGET BLINDNESS
======================================================================

Mechanism parameters, grammar model classes,
complexity penalties, nulls, distance functions,
support gates, and comparison rules must be frozen
before final target evaluation.

Exploratory VM work is permitted only when explicitly
separated from confirmatory evaluation.


======================================================================
42. EXPLORATORY / CONFIRMATORY SEPARATION
======================================================================

Phase III should explicitly contain:

    DISCOVERY

and:

    CONFIRMATION

stages.

Discovery may use training partitions.

Confirmatory partitions must remain untouched
until:

    model;
    parameters;
    metrics;
    nulls;
    thresholds;
    decision rules

are frozen.


======================================================================
43. NEGATIVE RESULTS
======================================================================

Phase III must treat the following as valid outcomes:

    no compact grammar exists;

    grammar predicts local structure
    but not manuscript hierarchy;

    residual remains highly structured;

    message-free generators reproduce F2;

    message-free generators fail F2;

    manuscript-scale mechanisms diverge more
    strongly from VM as coverage increases;

    generating classes remain non-identifiable.

No task should require a positive mechanism verdict.


======================================================================
44. CLAIM BOUNDARIES
======================================================================

A successful formal grammar does NOT establish:

    natural language;
    meaning;
    plaintext;
    authorship.

A low information residual does NOT establish:

    meaningless text.

A message-free generator matching F2 does NOT establish:

    VM is meaningless.

An external-memory model matching F2 does NOT establish:

    VM is external memory.

A natural-text model matching F2 does NOT establish:

    VM is natural language.

These are compatibility and discrimination experiments.


======================================================================
45. PRIMARY RESEARCH QUESTIONS
======================================================================

Phase III should answer, as far as evidence permits:

RQ1.
Can Voynichese be represented by a compact,
held-out validated formal grammar?

RQ2.
What is the minimum sufficient grammar?

RQ3.
How much information remains after conditioning
on that grammar?

RQ4.
Is the residual statistically structured?

RQ5.
Can message-free generators repeatedly reproduce
the same multi-family fingerprint?

RQ6.
Can competing mechanisms generate manuscript-scale
rather than token-stream structure?

RQ7.
How much of Fingerprint V2/V3 becomes directly
comparable after manuscript-scale generation?

RQ8.
Does expanded multi-family coverage make
generating classes identifiable?

RQ9.
Can formal grammar and message-free generation
be statistically distinguished?

RQ10.
Which observed VM properties remain unexplained
by every tested model?


======================================================================
46. SUCCESS CRITERIA
======================================================================

Phase III is scientifically successful even if
no origin class is identified.

Success requires obtaining reliable answers to:

    how compressible/predictable the formal structure is;

    whether a compact grammar exists;

    what residual remains;

    whether message-free reproduction is possible;

    whether manuscript-scale models can be built;

    whether direct fingerprint coverage expands;

    whether increased coverage increases discrimination.

======================================================================

Phase III should ultimately produce:

======================================================================
48. RELATION TO PREVIOUS PHASES
======================================================================

Phase I asked:

    What statistical structure does the VM have,
    and what broad mechanism classes remain possible?

Phase II asked:

    Is that structure compatible with
    external-memory, shorthand, extraction,
    natural, or null mechanisms?

and discovered:

    mechanism identification is blocked by
    narrow model/fingerprint intersection.

Phase III asks:

    What generative rules explain the structure,
    what information remains beyond those rules,
    and can mechanism classes be distinguished
    when they are required to reproduce the
    manuscript at its actual structural scale?

======================================================================
50. PHASE III FINAL QUESTION
======================================================================

The final question of Phase III is not:

    "What is the Voynich Manuscript?"

That remains too broad.

The final testable question is:

    Can the observable statistical and manuscript-scale
    structure of the Voynich Manuscript be explained by
    a compact generative model, and does the remaining
    evidence distinguish information-bearing,
    external-memory, natural-language, shorthand,
    selective-extraction, and message-free generating
    classes?

If the answer remains no, Phase III must determine why:

    insufficient measurement;

    insufficient model coverage;

    genuine statistical equifinality;

    or information inaccessible without semantics/context.
